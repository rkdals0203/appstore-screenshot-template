# Observe, represent, and compare

Read the complete source set before choosing a composition. Record the concrete
visual decisions that distinguish its treatments: typography and text density,
device pose and apparent scale, cropping, overlap, background light and colour,
decoration, and relationships across slides. Inspect relevant regions at native
resolution when the complete slide conceals an important detail.

For each distinct treatment, retain a short source/method note: which original
and region support the observation, which editable representation will express
it, and what will intentionally change. A familiar asset name, generic symbol,
or plausible default is not evidence that its appearance matches the source.

Choose native text, shapes, paths, devices, or image planes where supported.
Keep replaceable pixels separate from pose, clipping and hardware. Compare the
actual rendered treatment before treating an assumption as verified. Different
contours and poses require their own evidence, even if they share an object type.

Compare original and rendered compositions at equal displayed dimensions without
automatically aligning them to conceal placement differences. Use full-set views
for rhythm and hierarchy, and source-linked crops for distinguishing details.
Check typography, scale, pose, background distribution, overlap and decoration,
not only readability or absence of overflow. In adaptation, distinguish intended
changes to the user's content and brand from lost reference design features.

Locate a difference in source content, authored geometry, resource choice or
rendering before correcting it. Address concrete objects and compare the changed
result again. A render of an earlier revision cannot verify a later revision.
Record unresolved differences and the actual missing capability or evidence.
Technical validity and a model's favorable opinion do not certify design quality.
